What Is Roulette?

On Spin Crush, Roulette is a table game built around a wheel divided into numbered pockets and a layout where players place their chips. The wheel contains numbers from 1 to 36, plus one or two zero pockets. Numbers alternate between red and black, while zero is green. A small ball is spun around the wheel in the opposite direction, and the pocket where it settles determines the winning outcome for that round.

There are no complex hands or card dealing. Every round comes down to where the ball lands. Because the table offers many ways to bet, Roulette suits players who want to choose between safer, frequent wins and riskier, higher-paying wagers.

How Roulette Works

Every Roulette round follows the same basic rhythm: betting, spinning, and settlement.

Betting phase

Once the table is open, players place chips on the layout. The dealer signals when betting is closed, usually with a hand gesture or by saying "no more bets." Timing matters here: wagers placed after the cut-off are not accepted for that round.

The spin

The dealer spins the wheel in one direction and releases the ball along the track in the opposite direction. As the wheel loses momentum, the ball drops into one of the numbered pockets. Modern digital versions replicate this sequence using a certified random number generator, but the outcome concept is identical: a single winning pocket is selected.

Settlement

After the ball stops, the dealer announces the winning number and color. All losing chips are cleared from the table, and winning bets are paid according to their odds. Even-money bets such as red or black pay 1:1, while a straight-up bet on a single number typically pays 35:1. The table then resets for the next round.

Understanding the Roulette Table

The Roulette table layout groups every available bet into two main areas: the inside section and the outside section. Where you place your chips determines exactly what you are betting on.

Inside the table

The inner part of the layout shows the individual numbers arranged in a grid, along with their corresponding colors. This is where you place chips when you want to bet on specific numbers or small groups of adjacent numbers. The inside area also includes boxes for betting on zero or double zero where applicable.

Outside the table

The outer section offers larger groupings with better winning odds but lower payouts. Common outside areas include red or black, odd or even, and high (19–36) or low (1–18). Dozens and columns sit just outside the number grid, letting you bet on twelve numbers at once.

Key layout zones

  • Red / Black: Bet on whether the winning number will be red or black.
  • Odd / Even: Bet on whether the winning number will be odd or even.
  • High / Low: Bet on numbers 1–18 (low) or 19–36 (high).
  • Dozens: Bet on the first dozen (1–12), second dozen (13–24), or third dozen (25–36).
  • Columns: Bet on one of the three vertical columns on the layout, each covering twelve numbers.

Roulette Bet Types

Before placing any wager on Spin Crush, the key distinction is whether the bet covers a single number or a broader group. This split changes both your chances of winning and the payout you receive.

Inside Bets

Inside bets focus on specific numbers or tight clusters on the layout. Because they cover fewer pockets, they pay more when they hit.

  • Straight-up: A chip placed directly on a single number. This is the highest-paying standard inside bet.
  • Split: A chip placed on the line between two adjacent numbers, covering both.
  • Street: A chip placed at the end of a row of three numbers, covering the entire row.
  • Corner (or square): A chip placed at the intersection where four numbers meet, covering all four.
  • Line (or six-line): A chip placed at the boundary between two adjacent rows, covering six numbers.

Outside Bets

Outside bets cover larger groups of numbers. They win more often but offer smaller returns relative to the stake.

  • Red or Black: The ball lands on your chosen color.
  • Odd or Even: The ball lands on an odd or even number.
  • High or Low: The ball lands between 1 and 18 (low) or 19 and 36 (high).
  • Dozens: The ball lands in the first, second, or third block of twelve numbers.
  • Columns: The ball lands in one of the three vertical columns on the layout.

Roulette Payouts

On Spin Crush, payout tables are usually displayed on or near the table layout, showing what each winning bet returns. The general principle is consistent: narrower bets pay more because they cover fewer outcomes, while broader bets pay less because they win more frequently.

For example, even-money bets such as red or black usually return an amount equal to the stake. A bet on a single number pays substantially more, though it also carries a much lower probability of winning. Before playing, check the displayed payout table so you know exactly what each winning bet returns.

Keep in mind that zero (and double zero where present) changes the math. These pockets do not win for most outside bets, which is one reason the game includes a house edge even when the rules are otherwise fair.

Roulette Variants

Common Roulette variants differ mainly in the number of zero pockets on the wheel. This detail changes the house edge and affects which outside bets survive a zero result.

European Roulette

This version uses a single zero pocket. The wheel numbers are arranged so that red and black, odd and even, and high and low are spread evenly, with zero acting as the sole exception. Single zero Roulette is generally considered the standard form of the game in most parts of the world.

American Roulette

American Roulette adds a double zero pocket to the wheel, increasing the total number of pockets. The extra pocket gives the house a larger edge because it creates one more outcome that is not covered by most outside bets. The rest of the bet types and payout structure are largely the same as the single-zero version.

Other variants

Some versions introduce rule changes such as la partage or en prison, which modify what happens to even-money bets when the ball lands on zero. These rules can reduce the house edge on specific bet types, but they are not available on every table. Always check the rules displayed for the specific game you are playing.

Understanding Roulette Odds and Risk

Roulette odds are fixed by the wheel layout and the table rules. Every spin is independent, which means the result of one round has no effect on the next. Understanding this is one of the most useful things a player can do before placing money on the table.

Why payouts differ

A bet that covers fewer outcomes must pay more when it wins, because it is statistically less likely to succeed. Conversely, a bet that covers many outcomes wins more often but returns less per win. This trade-off is the core risk-reward structure of the game.

Inside versus outside

Inside bets give you more potential return per spin, but they also expose you to longer losing streaks. Outside bets smooth out results because they win more frequently, though individual wins are smaller. Neither type is inherently better; they simply serve different risk tolerances.

Independence of spins

Each Roulette spin starts from the same conditions. The wheel and ball do not remember whether red appeared ten times in a row, nor do they adjust to "balance things out." Strategies that rely on previous results to predict future outcomes misunderstand how the game actually works.

Roulette FAQs

What is Roulette?

Roulette is a table game where players bet on where a small ball will land on a spinning wheel. The wheel has numbered pockets, and each round ends when the ball settles into one of them. Bets that match the result win according to set payout rules.

How does Roulette work?

A round begins with a betting phase where players place chips on the table layout. The dealer then spins the wheel and releases the ball in the opposite direction. When the ball stops, the winning number and color are announced, and losing bets are collected while winning bets are paid out.

What is the difference between inside and outside bets?

Inside bets are placed on specific numbers or small groups of numbers on the inner section of the table. They offer higher payouts because they cover fewer outcomes. Outside bets are placed on broader categories such as red or black, odd or even, or ranges of numbers. They win more often but pay less.

What is a straight-up bet?

A straight-up bet is a wager placed on a single number. It is the riskiest common bet in Roulette because it only covers one pocket, but it also pays the highest standard return among the basic bet types.

What is the difference between European and American Roulette?

European Roulette uses a wheel with a single zero pocket, while American Roulette adds a double zero pocket. The extra pocket in American Roulette increases the house edge, making European Roulette statistically more favorable to players when both options are available.

What does zero mean in Roulette?

Zero is a special pocket on the Roulette wheel. In most games, zero is neither red nor black, and it is not included in odd, even, high, or low bets. When the ball lands on zero, most outside bets lose, which is one reason the game has a built-in house edge.

Are previous Roulette results useful for predicting the next spin?

No. Each Roulette spin is independent of the ones before it. The ball and wheel have no memory, so past results do not influence future outcomes. Any pattern perceived in previous spins is purely coincidental.

How do Roulette payouts work?

Payouts in Roulette are determined by how many pockets a bet covers. A bet on a single number pays more than a bet on red or black because it is less likely to win. Payout tables are usually displayed on the table layout, and winning bets are paid automatically after the result is confirmed.

Can Roulette outcomes be predicted?

No. Roulette outcomes are generated by a physical wheel and ball (or a certified random number generator in digital versions), making each result independent and unpredictable. No system, pattern, or betting strategy can guarantee accurate predictions.
